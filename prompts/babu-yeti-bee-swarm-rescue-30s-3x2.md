# Babu Yeti Bee Swarm Rescue 30s Panel Images and 3x2 Grids

Image prompts for `prompts/babu-yeti-bee-swarm-rescue-30s-storyboard.md`, which holds the video prompts. Each 10-second video has, under its own heading, six panel image prompts, one per beat of its storyboard table, timed and framed by the panel map in section 6 of `.agents/rules/cinematic-direction.md`. Each draws its panel's picture as a full single image with the full character lock of each Yeti in it from `.agents/rules/character-consistency.md`, the shot's light, and the panel's faces in its `Expressions:` sentence, as sections 2 to 4 of `.agents/rules/prompt-assembly.md` set out. Each video's 3x2 grid is composed from its six panel images by `compose_grid.ps1`; it has no prompt and is never drawn by an image model, so the grid and the panel images are the same six pictures.

18 panel images and three grids in total, all in `3x2-timed-storyboard-images/babu-yeti-bee-swarm-rescue/`: each panel image as `Panel_<P>_V<N>.jpg` and each grid as `Grid_V<N>.jpg`, where `V` is the video number. The panel images are vertical 9:16, and each grid is a 1920x1080 horizontal canvas.

To make a video's images:

1. Generate the six panel images one prompt at a time, each with the family reference image attached as a character reference, the prompt pasted whole, and the output set to vertical 9:16. Save each under the name its `Image:` line gives.
2. Check every panel image against its prompt and against the look check in section 7 of `.agents/rules/prompt-assembly.md`, which starts from the Final Consistency Checklist in `.agents/rules/character-consistency.md`: Babu Yeti is the only Yeti in this film, in vibrant mint-green fur with his single curly tuft, about half the height of a grown-up Yeti, wearing nothing and with nothing on his head, with large expressive eyes, small button nose, and square clean teeth. Papa Yeti and Mama Yeti never appear in any panel. The supporting humans are exactly two young children (a boy in a green tunic and a girl in a yellow dress). No text, captions, or watermarks appear. Then check the six side by side for the same look across the set. Regenerate any image that fails; a wrong image is cheaper to redo than a wrong clip.
3. Checks for this film:
   - Babu Yeti appears in Panels 3 to 6 of Video 1, and all panels of Videos 2 and 3. Panels 1 and 2 of Video 1 feature only the two children and the approaching bee swarm.
   - Papa Yeti and Mama Yeti do not appear in this film.
   - Babu Yeti holds a slender polished bamboo stick; no paws or props are ever above his head without lifting away immediately.
   - The bee swarm disperses into harmless shimmering golden pollen dust; no energy beams, auras, blasts, or superhero power-ups appear.
4. Compose the grid from the six saved panel images: `pwsh -File .agents/skills/create-3x2-timed-image/compose_grid.ps1 -File prompts/babu-yeti-bee-swarm-rescue-30s-3x2.md -Video <N>`. Compose it again whenever a panel image changes.
5. If the image model refuses a prompt, the filter causes in the storyboard's agent instructions apply to it too. Change only that prompt's picture and effects text and keep every lock, character locks included.

To use them in Google Flow, set the output format to vertical 9:16, generate the video's clip in ingredients-to-video mode with the video's `Character locks:` block and table from the storyboard pasted as the prompt, and attach one of these:

- Panel route: the six panel images and the family reference image. That is seven images, the most Flow accepts for one clip, and it gives Flow every beat of the clip at full size.
- Grid route: the composed grid and the family reference image.

Never attach more than seven images, and never set a panel image or a grid as a start or an end frame. Check the clip against the six panel images, each at its timecode.
