# Babu Yeti and the Sour Mango Trick 150s Storyboard

In a lush green forest valley, selfish Kobi Squirrel keeps every mango of a magnificent tree to himself while Pied Goat and the Woolly Hares go hungry. Babu Yeti hatches a clever plan with Papa Yeti: tell Kobi Squirrel his mangoes are sour, and pull terrible faces at every one he throws down to prove them sweet. Kobi Squirrel shakes the whole tree bare in a temper and glides off in search of a sweeter tree, and the friends feast. When Mama Yeti asks what Kobi Squirrel will eat, Babu Yeti shares the biggest mango with him as he creeps back hungry, and everyone eats together in the shade. The tone is playful and comic, rising to a cross, fast-paced climax, then softening to tender and warm. The moral: mangoes taste sweetest when shared.

The film is 15 videos of 10 seconds each, 150 seconds in all, vertical 9:16, adapted from the user's draft fable with the `story-to-storyboard` skill. Each clip is generated in Google Flow with its own narration, dialogue, music, and sound effects. Everything in the video workflow is written for the Google Flow Agent: three instruction cards, one setup prompt, and one Agent prompt per video. The Image map and the `Images:` lines say which images go in the prompt box for each paste. The saved Characters @Papa Yeti, @Mama Yeti, @Babu Yeti, @Kobi Squirrel, @Woolly Hare, and @Pied Goat carry their looks, so nothing here describes them. Two separate promotional cover prompts follow the final video.

## Story adaptation

| Draft character | Role in the plot | Film character | Why |

| (none) | The voice of kindness | Mama Yeti | Asks the question that turns the ending from a win to a share |
| The storyteller | Narration | Narrator, off-screen | Opens with "Once upon a time", covers the montage and the time jump, speaks the moral |

Changed or added, and why:

- No fox, crow, or monkey appears anywhere. Their lines are kept, renamed, and trimmed to fit each clip ("Brother Monkey" becomes "Kobi").
- The setting moves to a forest valley in the Himalayan foothills with snowy peaks behind, so the Yetis belong in it. The mango tree and its golden mangoes stay.
- The heroes' trick now has a reason that helps others: hungry Pied Goat and the Woolly Hares cannot reach the fruit, and Kobi Squirrel shoos them away (Video 2).
- "Whatever I say, nod and agree" becomes a secret whisper behind a mossy boulder, and the repeated tastings become a comic run of grimaces (Videos 6 to 9).
- The monkey's rage and departure become Kobi Squirrel shaking the tree bare and gliding off into the jungle. Nobody is hurt, and the fruit lands in soft grass (Videos 10 and 11).
- The draft ends with the tricksters feasting. The film adds a kindness beat: Mama Yeti asks what Kobi Squirrel will eat, and Babu Yeti shares the biggest mango when he creeps back hungry (Videos 13 to 15). This keeps the Yetis heroes and gives the fable a moral for young viewers.
- The whole story takes place in one sunny day, with the light warming to golden afternoon for the ending.

Moral, spoken by the Narrator in Video 15: "Mangoes taste sweetest when shared."
