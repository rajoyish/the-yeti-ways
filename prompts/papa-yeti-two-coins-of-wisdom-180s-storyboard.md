# Papa Yeti and the Two Coins of Wisdom 180s Storyboard

In a little Himalayan village, humble Papa Yeti and his family have almost nothing, while their clever neighbour Tashi Marmot is very rich. Papa Yeti spends the family's two saved coins on two pieces of wisdom, one from sly Blue Magpie, who means to trick him, and one from kind Dolma Yak. Obeying three haughty Grand Peacocks, he buries their "smelly old bundle", and it turns out to be treasure. When jealous Tashi Marmot accuses him before Snow Leopard Queen, Papa Yeti tells her the whole truth and is forgiven. Back home, he forgives Tashi Marmot in turn and shares the family supper. The tone is gentle and warm with comic touches, tense before the Queen, then tender. The moral: an honest heart is the greatest treasure.

The film is 18 videos of 10 seconds each, 180 seconds in all, vertical 9:16, adapted from the user's draft folk tale with the `story-to-storyboard` skill. Each clip is generated in Google Flow with its own narration, dialogue, music, and sound effects. Everything in the video workflow is written for the Google Flow Agent: three instruction cards, one setup prompt, and one Agent prompt per video. The Image map and the `Images:` lines say which images go in the prompt box for each paste. The saved Characters @Papa Yeti, @Mama Yeti, @Babu Yeti, @Tashi Marmot, @Blue Magpie, @Dolma Yak, @Grand Peacock, @Snow Leopard Queen, and @Woolly Hare carry their looks, so nothing here describes them, except where a video has more cast than its 3 linked Characters: there the Yetis are always linked, and the story cast without a slot (the Woolly Hares in Video 13, Tashi Marmot in Video 18) is described in that video's `Cast:` line. Two separate promotional cover prompts follow the final video.

| The storyteller | Narration | Narrator, off-screen | Opens with "Once upon a time", carries the walk, the magpie's secret plan, the memories of each wisdom, and the moral |

Changed or added, and why:

- No human appears anywhere: every draft character is now an animal or a Yeti, and no draft name, "king", "brother", or "shopkeeper" is used in the film. The two coins replace the two rupees.
- The two brothers become two neighbours, because a second grown-up Yeti would be a fourth Yeti and a Yeti never plays the jealous rival.
- The draft calls the hero foolish. In the film Papa Yeti only says "I'm not so clever": he is humble, not a fool, and the magpie's would-be trick turns out to be good advice. His honesty proves the wisest choice of all.
- The dead body becomes a bundle of cloth that the Grand Peacocks think is smelly rubbish, so nothing in the film shows or names a death. The surprise and the white cloth stay (Videos 8 to 11).
- "Bury this corpse" becomes "Bury it, and take these coins!", and Papa Yeti digs the pit with a wooden spade from a handcart.
- The accusation of stealing becomes "My neighbour took your treasure!", and the King's summons is shown as a firm knock at the cottage door (Video 14).
- Added from the story's own world: the village lane with Tashi Marmot's grand house beside the family's humble cottage, the little wooden money box, Babu Yeti waving Papa off and welcoming him home, and one day of light from morning to lantern-lit dusk.
- Added kindness beat: Tashi Marmot says sorry, Papa Yeti forgives him and invites him to supper, and Babu Yeti hands him a bowl of rice (Videos 17 and 18). The Narrator speaks the moral once, in Video 18.

Moral, spoken by the Narrator in Video 18: "An honest heart is the greatest treasure."
