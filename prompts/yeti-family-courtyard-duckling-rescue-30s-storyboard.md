# Yeti Family Courtyard Duckling Rescue 30s Storyboard

On a quiet afternoon in a Himalayan village courtyard, a gust tips a brazier of coals into the straw and rings a tiny duckling in low flames against a cart wheel, until Babu Yeti spots it, Papa Yeti sweeps the burning straw away with his wooden staff, and Mama Yeti douses the coals so the duckling can run back to its family, tense at first and then warm and gently funny as Papa Yeti turns bashful about it. It has been strictly formatted for AI video generation according to character styling rules, environment locks, cinematic direction, and social media prompt guidelines.

## Flow setup

This storyboard is three 10-second Google Flow videos that cut together into one 30-second film, made in a new Flow project with the saved Yeti Characters, as `.agents/rules/google-flow.md` sets out. Each video has a table, the master its panel images are built from, then an `Ingredients:` line and a `Flow prompt:` block, the only text Flow receives for that clip. The six checked panel images for each video are in `prompts/yeti-family-courtyard-duckling-rescue-30s-3x2.md` under the same `## Video N - <Title>` heading.

1. New project named "Yeti Family Courtyard Duckling Rescue". Settings: Omni Flash (Gemini Omni Flash 1.1), Video, Ingredients, vertical 9:16, 10 seconds, 720p. "Return silent videos" off. Turn the Agent on and, in its Settings, set the default video model to Omni Flash, orientation vertical, and "Confirm before generating" to Always.
2. Characters: type `@` and check `@Papa Yeti`, `@Mama Yeti`, and `@Babu Yeti` resolve to the saved Characters. The three ducks are not Characters; each Flow prompt describes them.
3. Plates: generate `Plate - Courtyard` from the `Environment lock:`, as section 3 of `google-flow.md` says.
4. Agent instructions: add the three cards under "Google Flow Agent instructions", each with its title, reference image, and guideline, toggled on.
5. Upload `Family height chart` (`references/characters/family-height-chart.png`): Babu Yeti shares shots with his parents in all three videos.

Then, for each video in order: attach the ingredients on its `Ingredients:` line, paste its `Flow prompt:` block (with the Agent on, add the line under it), and check, upscale, save `End frame V<N>`, and assemble in Scenebuilder as section 4 of `google-flow.md` says. Compare each clip with its six panel images at their timecodes. No video opens a new scene.

Every Flow prompt runs on its own 00:00 - 00:10 clock; the `Film position:` and `Stage:` lines are for the operator and the editor, never for Flow. If a clip comes out the wrong length, check the model and the length setting first. If a clip needs a change, change the table first, then rewrite the Flow prompt from it and regenerate any panel image the change touches. If Flow refuses a clip, ask the Agent what triggered it (the `Clip rules` card tells it how to answer) and check its rewrite keeps the timestamps, the `@` names, the style sentence, and the Avoid line. If it cannot clear it, retry one change at a time and stop at the first that passes: (1) replace the Sound line with "Soft background music. No dialogue, no lyrics."; (2) remove the effect wording; (3) cut each shot to its main physical action; (4) remove the Avoid line from the clip prompt only; (5) remove the panel image guide. Copy any change that passes back into the table, and note the trigger here so the next story avoids it.

Likely filter trigger in this film: the small flames around the cart wheel in Video 1 and the start of Video 2. They are written as low, small flames in spilled straw that never touch the duckling or a Yeti, and the duckling's face is worried, not terrified. If the safety filter still refuses Video 1 or Video 2, soften the flames to "smouldering straw with thin wisps of smoke and a soft orange glow" in both videos, so the handoff still matches.

The tables predate the saved Characters and still say "paw" in places; the Flow prompts say "five-fingered hand", which is what the Characters show.
