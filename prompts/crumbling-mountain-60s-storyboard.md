# The Crumbling Mountain 60s Storyboard

A year of unnatural warmth has thawed the ice that holds the peaks together. The Yeti family feels it first in their cave, and when a section of the far glacier shears away and turns into a debris flood, they race down to the valley and lift its villagers and birds onto a ledge seconds before it hits. The point is that the warming mountains are fragile, and that the Yetis, the guardians of the peaks, are both the rescuers and the warning. It has been strictly formatted for AI video generation according to character styling rules, environment locks, and social media prompt guidelines. There is no dialogue; every line from the original story is carried by gesture, expression, and sound.

## Agent instructions

This storyboard is six 10-second Google Flow videos that cut together into one 60-second film. Follow these steps in order.

1. Set Flow's output format to vertical 9:16 before generating anything, and check it again before each clip. Flow falls back to 16:9 on a new session.
2. Generate Video 1 from its three shots as one 10-second clip. Paste the three table rows in order as a single prompt; do not generate them separately.
3. Check the clip is vertical 9:16 and that its final frame matches the `End frame:` line under the Video 1 table. If either is wrong, regenerate Video 1 before continuing.
4. Generate each following video by extending from the final frame of the one before it, using that video's table as the prompt. Repeat both checks after every clip.
5. Video 3 starts a new scene. Do not extend it. Generate it fresh from its own prompt, matching the `Scene break:` line under the Video 2 table. Confirm the output format is still 9:16, because a fresh generation is where it most often reverts.
6. Cut the six clips together in order with no transitions. Keep the music continuous across the cuts.

Every video's table runs on its own 00:00 - 00:10 clock. Paste one table into Flow at a time, and paste only the table. The `Film position:` line under each heading is where the clip sits in the finished 60-second cut; it is for the editor, not for Flow. If Flow produces more than one clip, a clip longer than 10 seconds, or a timing error, the prompt contained timestamps above 00:10. Remove them and retry.

Every visual cell opens with `Vertical 9:16 aspect ratio, full-frame vertical composition.` Keep that line in the prompt even when Flow's output format is already set to 9:16. The setting and the line together are what stop it reverting to widescreen. If a clip renders 16:9, regenerate it with the format reset rather than cropping, because cropping throws away the top and bottom of the framing.

Do not change the aspect ratio line, style lock, environment sentences, head lock, character descriptions, or the `Humans:` and `Animals:` sentences in any prompt. Only the `Action:` and audio text differ between shots. If Flow rejects a clip, read the message. "This prompt may violate our policies" is the safety filter, and the cause is almost always a brand, a real device, a named stock sound effect, on-screen text, or dialogue. "I can't generate the video you requested right now due to interests of third-party content providers" is the copyright filter, and the cause is a name, a style, a song, or an `Action:` sentence that reads like a famous film moment. Hand the prompt to Flow's agent first: it can see what the filter matched, which the message does not say. Give it this request, with the refused prompt pasted under it:

> This prompt was refused with "I can't generate the video you requested right now due to interests of third-party content providers." Find what triggered the refusal and rewrite the prompt so it passes. Keep the three rows and their timestamps (00:00 - 00:03, 00:03 - 00:07, 00:07 - 00:10). Change only the `Action:` sentences and the audio text. Do not change, shorten, or reorder the aspect ratio line, the style lock, the `Environment:` sentence, the character descriptions, the `Head lock:` sentence, the `Humans:` sentence, or the `Animals:` sentence. Keep every story beat, keep the cello as the only instrument, keep "No dialogue." at the end of each audio cell, and tell me what you changed and why.

Check the agent's rewrite before generating: every locked sentence must still be there word for word, no timestamp may be above 00:10, and every audio cell must end with "No dialogue." If the agent touched a lock, paste the original lock back over its version. Once the rewritten prompt passes, copy its `Action:` and audio changes back into this storyboard so the later videos are written against what was actually generated, and note what the agent said the trigger was so the next storyboard avoids it.

If the agent cannot clear it, or there is no agent in the session, work through these retries in order, one change at a time, and stop at the first that passes: (1) replace every audio cell with "Soft background music. No dialogue."; (2) cut each `Action:` to its first sentence; (3) download the final frame of the previous clip as an image and generate this video from that frame instead of with extend, using the same prompt, because extend sends the previous clip along with the prompt and the filter judges both. Never change the character text.

Aspect ratio lock: vertical 9:16 for every clip, set in Flow's output format and stated in the first line of every prompt.

Head lock: no Yeti has horns, antlers, spikes, or anything on its head besides its tuft of fur. Every prompt says so after the character text. If a clip comes back with anything on a Yeti's head, regenerate that clip before extending from it. A horned frame passed to the next clip carries the horns through the rest of the film. Nobody in this film raises arms over the head: every lift, point, sweep, and shielding gesture is at chest height, the man's awe is one hand flat on his chest, and his thanks is both hands pressed together at his chest. The villagers wear no hats, so nothing on a head in the frame can carry over to a Yeti.

Human lock: a family of three stylized 3D animated cartoon villagers with rounded, friendly faces and warm brown skin, a man and a woman in simple hand-woven earth-toned tunics tied with cloth belts, and a small child in a matching tunic held against the woman's chest; they wear no hats, and their clothes carry no logos, patterns with writing, or modern gear. The `Humans:` sentence is copied word for word into every prompt, including the cave prompts of Videos 1 and 2, where the villagers are not on screen. They appear only in Videos 3 to 6. In Video 3 they are on the valley floor by the huts and the pink Yeti signals them toward the ledge. In Video 4 the blue Yeti lifts the man and the pink Yeti lifts the woman with the child onto the ledge. In Videos 5 and 6 they stay on the ledge behind the Yetis. The child stays against the woman's chest in every shot, never touches the mud, and is never alone. No villager ever falls, enters water, or is touched by the surge. Their expressions are worry and then awe, never terror.

Animal lock: a flock of six small round grey-brown mountain birds with soft feathers, pale breasts, short beaks, and expressive cartoon eyes. The `Animals:` sentence is copied word for word into every prompt, including the cave prompts of Videos 1 and 2, where the birds are not on screen. They appear only in Videos 3 to 6. In Video 3 they peck at the grass beside the footbridge and startle when the Yetis arrive. In Video 4 the baby Yeti herds them across a low flat rock and up the grassy ramp onto the ledge. In Videos 5 and 6 they huddle and then settle on the rock beside him. No bird is ever caught by the surge.

Prop lock: inside the cave, one ice wall with a deep weeping fracture, thin trickles of meltwater, and a puddle of meltwater on the stone floor by the inner ledge. In the valley, a cluster of small stone huts with slate roofs and plain wooden doors, one wooden footbridge of rough planks over a shallow stream, one dirt path, one low flat rock at the foot of the grassy ramp, and one bundle of firewood tied with cord that the man carries in Video 3 and drops in its final shot. The surge is brown mud, broken ice, and rounded boulders; it carries off the footbridge, buries the path, piles against the hut walls, and stops at the foot of the ramp, and it never reaches the ledge. No signs, no writing, no tools, no devices, no brands, labels, or packaging. No dialogue in any shot.

Environment lock: Videos 1 and 2 use Environment: A quiet, snow-dusted rock cave retreat high in the Himalayas, its ice-glazed walls streaked with thin trickles of meltwater, with a wide cave mouth opening onto a broad snowy slope and a distant white glacier face under a heavy grey sky. Videos 3 to 6 use Environment: A green Himalayan valley floor with a cluster of small stone huts with slate roofs, a wooden footbridge over a shallow stream, a dirt path leading up-valley toward a rocky gorge, and a broad low rock ledge with a grassy ramp rising a few steps above the valley floor, under a heavy grey sky. The break in the clouds and the warm light in Video 6 are written in the `Action:` text, not in the environment sentence, which stays as written.
