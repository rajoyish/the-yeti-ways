# Yeti Family 42-Form Tai Chi 70s Storyboard

Papa Yeti, Mama Yeti, and Babu Yeti embark on a meditative martial arts journey across seven breathtaking mountain sanctuaries, practicing and mastering the complete, traditional 42-Form Tai Chi routine in perfect family unison. Following the authentic competition sequence, every form is choreographed step-by-step from commencement to completion. Papa Yeti grounds the movements with strong, centered poise; Mama Yeti flows with graceful, fluid spiral power; and Babu Yeti mirrors his parents with earnest focus and playful determination. Across seventy seconds divided into seven 10-second Google Flow videos, each distinct natural setting frames six sequential Tai Chi forms—from the opening breath on a misty stone terrace to the final peaceful closing stance at sunset upon the mountain crest. It has been strictly formatted for AI video generation according to character styling rules, environment locks, cinematic direction, and social media prompt guidelines. There is no dialogue; every posture and emotion is carried by deliberate movement, disciplined breathing, and the soulful melody of a solo Erhu.

## Agent instructions

This storyboard is seven 10-second Google Flow videos that cut together into one 70-second martial arts film. The file holds the video prompts only: each video's `Character locks:` block and table, pasted together, are the text prompt for one Flow generation. The image prompts for this film, each video's six panel images and the 3x2 grid composed from them, will reside in `prompts/yeti-family-42-form-tai-chi-70s-3x2.md` under matching `## Video N - <Title>` headings. No clip starts or ends on a supplied frame.

Follow these steps in order:

1. Before generating a clip, generate its video's six panel images, verify them against character locks, and compose its grid from them, as `prompts/yeti-family-42-form-tai-chi-70s-3x2.md` specifies. If that file does not exist, run `/create-3x2-timed-image` on this storyboard to write it. Do not generate a clip without its reference images.
2. Set Flow's output format to vertical 9:16 before generating any clip, and verify it again before each generation. Flow defaults to 16:9 on a new session.
3. Generate Video 1 in Flow's ingredients-to-video mode. Attach the Video 1 images for your selected route: the six panel images (`Panel_1_V1.jpg` to `Panel_6_V1.jpg`) and the family reference image (seven images total, Flow's maximum limit), or the composed grid (`Grid_V1.jpg`) and the family reference image. Paste the Video 1 `Character locks:` block and then its three table rows, in order, as one prompt, VFX cells included. Do not generate rows separately, do not leave locks out, and do not set a start or end frame.
4. Check the clip: vertical 9:16, matching its video's grid panels and panel images in martial arts posture, step execution, paw placement, expression, color, tuft, scale, framing, and light. No Yeti changes color, grows or shrinks, or gains headwear or accessories. Paws and arms remain at or below chest/shoulder height—never raised directly overhead. The camera moves as directed, and no effects appear beyond those specified. If drift occurs, regenerate the clip before proceeding.
5. Generate Videos 2 through 7 using each video's respective images, `Character locks:` block, and table. Because each 10-second video transitions to a new environment, Videos 2 through 7 are marked with `Scene break:` and open fresh in their respective scenic landscapes.
6. Cut the seven clips together in chronological order with clean hard cuts between scenes. Maintain continuous audio across the cuts. Apply a consistent cinematic color grade matched to the palette in the `Lighting lock:` paragraph. Do not retime any clip; each remains exactly 10 seconds.

Every video table runs on its own 00:00 - 00:10 clock. Paste one video into Flow at a time: its `Character locks:` block and its table, and nothing else. The `Film position:` and `Stage:` lines are for production tracking.

Every visual cell opens with `Vertical 9:16 aspect ratio, full-frame vertical composition.` Keep that line intact to prevent accidental reversion to widescreen.
