# Babu Yeti Wolf Path Rescue 30s Panel Images and 3x2 Grids

Image prompts for `prompts/babu-yeti-wolf-path-rescue-30s-storyboard.md`, which holds the video prompts. Each 10-second video has, under its own heading, six panel image prompts, one per beat of its storyboard table, timed and framed by the panel map in section 6 of `.agents/rules/cinematic-direction.md`. Each draws its panel's picture as a full single image with Babu Yeti's full character lock from `.agents/rules/character-consistency.md` wherever he is in the picture, the shot's light, and his face in an `Expressions:` sentence, as sections 2 to 4 of `.agents/rules/prompt-assembly.md` set out. Each video's 3x2 grid is composed from its six panel images by `compose_grid.ps1`; it has no prompt and is never drawn by an image model, so the grid and the panel images are the same six pictures.

18 panel images and three grids in total, all in `3x2-timed-storyboard-images/babu-yeti-wolf-path-rescue/`: each panel image as `Panel_<P>_V<N>.jpg` and each grid as `Grid_V<N>.jpg`, where `V` is the video number. The panel images are vertical 9:16, and each grid is a 1920x1080 horizontal canvas.

To make a video's images:

1. Generate the six panel images one prompt at a time, each with the family reference image attached as a character reference, the prompt pasted whole, and the output set to vertical 9:16. Save each under the name its `Image:` line gives.
2. Check every panel image against its prompt and against the look check in section 7 of `.agents/rules/prompt-assembly.md`, which starts from the Final Consistency Checklist in `.agents/rules/character-consistency.md`: Babu Yeti the same individual in every panel, mint green with his single curl, nothing worn and nothing on any head, no extra Yeti, the face the prompt names, the framing, angle, and light the prompt names, every listed character and animal present in the stated number and no one else, and no text. Then check the six side by side for the same look across the set. Regenerate any image that fails; a wrong image is cheaper to redo than a wrong clip.
3. Checks for this film:
   - Babu Yeti is the only Yeti in the film. He is in Video 1 panel 1, Video 2 panels 2 to 5, and Video 3 panels 5 and 6, and in no other image. The family reference image shows all three Yetis, and his lock sizes him against a grown-up Yeti and names the blue and pink Yetis' tufts in its closing clause, so the model may add a blue or pink Yeti; regenerate any image that has one. For his panels, his own full-body portrait works as the character reference in place of the family reference image, and it carries no other Yeti.
   - The pet is a small ginger tabby cat: bright orange with cream stripes, a white chest and paws, green eyes, and a long striped tail. It is never a dog, never grey, and never anywhere near a wolf's size. The earlier dog version of this film failed because the model could not tell a brown dog from the wolves, so regenerate any image where the cat looks like a dog or a wolf cub. The cat rides on the bundle of sticks in Video 1 panels 2 to 4, is mid-leap in panel 5, stands arched and hissing in Video 1 panel 6 and Video 2 panel 1, is hugged in Video 2 panel 6 and Video 3 panels 1 and 2, and rides on the woodcutter's shoulder in Video 3 panels 3, 4, and 6. It wears no collar.
   - The wolves are in Video 1 panels 3 to 6 and Video 2 panels 1, 3, 4, and 5 (only their tails, vanishing into the pines, in panel 5), always exactly three. No wolf is in any Video 3 image. The wolves' mouths stay closed and show no teeth in every image, and no wolf touches the woodcutter, the cat, or Babu Yeti.
   - The woodcutter is a stylized cartoon grown man of about thirty with a short, neat dark beard and an adult's height, never a child or a boy and never photoreal, bare-headed with no hat, cap, or hood, and the bundle of sticks stays on his back in every image he is in. The first sets of Video 1 images gave him a knitted cap and then drew him as a boy; regenerate any image that does either.
   - The dragon is a cloud, in Video 2 panels 3 and 4 only: grey-white cloud high in the sky above the pines with gold edges from the sun behind it. Regenerate any image where it glows from inside, looks like a living creature with eyes or scales, sits close behind Babu Yeti, or seems to come out of him. The golden light in those panels is sunlight falling from above, never light coming from Babu Yeti.
   - The only lights are the sun and the sky. The family reference image has lanterns hanging in its trees, and an earlier panel image copied them; regenerate any image with a lantern, a lamp, or any glow that is not sunlight.
   - Babu Yeti holds his apples at chest height; nothing is ever on or above his head. He shows no smile of any kind in Video 2 panels 2 to 4, and no expression changes his face shape, eye size, or muzzle.
   - The Video 1 images made before the cat revision show the brown dog and the woodcutter's cap, and they came from the old names. They were moved to `3x2-timed-storyboard-images/babu-yeti-wolf-path-rescue/_superseded/` and must not be used.
   - Every prompt below now carries Babu Yeti's full character lock in place of the older character text and head and look locks. Any panel image generated from the older prompts is kept only if it passes the look check against the current lock; otherwise regenerate it from the prompt below and compose its grid again.
4. Compose the grid from the six saved panel images: `pwsh -File .agents/skills/create-3x2-timed-image/compose_grid.ps1 -File prompts/babu-yeti-wolf-path-rescue-30s-3x2.md -Video <N>`. Compose it again whenever a panel image changes.
5. If the image model refuses a prompt, the filter causes in the storyboard's agent instructions apply to it too. Change only that prompt's picture and effects text and keep every lock, Babu Yeti's character lock included. For Video 2 panels 3 and 4, the dragon cloud is the first thing to take out, as the storyboard's agent instructions describe.

To use them in Google Flow, set the output format to vertical 9:16, generate the video's clip in ingredients-to-video mode with the video's `Character locks:` block and table from the storyboard pasted as the prompt, and attach one of these:

- Panel route: the six panel images and the family reference image. That is seven images, the most Flow accepts for one clip, and it gives Flow every beat of the clip at full size.
- Grid route: the composed grid and the family reference image.

Never attach more than seven images, and never set a panel image or a grid as a start or an end frame. Check the clip against the six panel images, each at its timecode.
