# Tashi Marmot Flow Character

A plump, clever, rich Himalayan marmot who lives next door to the Yeti family, grows jealous of their luck, and learns to say sorry. Built from the story cast of the Two Coins of Wisdom storyboard in the stylized 3D style of the Yeti films.

Source: `prompts/papa-yeti-two-coins-of-wisdom-180s-16x9-storyboard.md` (Videos 1, 13, 14, and 16 to 18; the same cast as `prompts/papa-yeti-two-coins-of-wisdom-180s-storyboard.md`). Linked as a Character in Videos 1, 13, 14, 16, and 17; described-only in Video 18, where all three Yetis take the Character slots.

Draft changes: every trait from the storyboard's `Cast:` description is kept (plump, glossy golden-tan fur, dark brown muzzle and crown, cream belly, small round ears, quick dark eyes, two small square front teeth, short bushy dark-tipped tail, crimson embroidered silk vest with gold buttons and no writing). Gaps filled:
- Dark brown irises with bright catchlights, and short, expressive dark brown brows.
- A small, rounded dark brown nose with fine dark whiskers.
- The vest's embroidery: small gold swirls along its edges, no letters or symbols; three round gold buttons down the front.
- Small dark brown hands on the front legs with four short, separate fingers each (he polishes, holds, grips, and clasps with them in the shots), and broad back feet with five short toes each, no visible claws.
- A confident, slightly smug closed-mouth smile at rest, friendly rather than mean; the films' acting supplies his jealousy.
- He stands upright on his back legs, as every shot shows. His size (about the height of a small barrel) stays in the storyboard's `Cast:` line.

## Character info

```text
Tashi Marmot is a plump, clever, wealthy Himalayan marmot who lives in a grand painted house next door to the Yeti family. He is proud of his riches, quick-witted, and a little vain, polishing his gold coins until they shine, and he can turn jealous and huffy when someone else has good luck. Underneath he is not cruel, and when he is shown kindness he is quick to feel ashamed and say sorry. He stands and walks upright on his back feet in quick, bustling steps, wrings or clasps his small hands when nervous, raises his chin when pleased with himself, and flattens his ears when ashamed. He makes small sniffs and huffs. Around the Yetis he starts out envious and ends up grateful and friendly. He stands about the height of a small barrel.
```
