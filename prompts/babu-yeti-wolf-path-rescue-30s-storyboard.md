# Babu Yeti Wolf Path Rescue 30s Storyboard

A young woodcutter walks home along a snowy pine path with his ginger cat riding on his bundle of sticks, while Babu Yeti munches an apple high in the tallest pine. Three grey wolves come down the path, the woodcutter stumbles into the snow, and the little cat leaps down and puffs itself up to guard him, until Babu Yeti drops from the pine into a shaft of breaking sunlight, while the parting cloud above curls into the shape of a golden-edged dragon, and faces the wolves down. The wolves flee, the woodcutter hugs his cat in relief, and when he finally looks up, Babu Yeti is back on his branch and drops him an apple. The tone runs from quiet unease to brave and tense, then to tender and warm. It has been strictly formatted for AI video generation according to character styling rules, environment locks, cinematic direction, and social media prompt guidelines.

## Agent instructions

This storyboard is three 10-second Google Flow videos that cut together into one 30-second scene. The file holds the video prompts only: each video's `Character locks:` block and table, pasted together, are the text prompt for one Flow generation. The images each clip is generated with, the video's six panel images and the 3x2 grid composed from them, are listed in `prompts/babu-yeti-wolf-path-rescue-30s-3x2.md` under the same `## Video N - <Title>` heading, with the steps for generating and checking them. No clip starts or ends on a supplied frame.

Follow these steps in order.

1. Before generating a clip, make its video's images as `prompts/babu-yeti-wolf-path-rescue-30s-3x2.md` says: the six panel images first, then the grid composed from those same six files. If that file does not exist, run `/create-3x2-timed-image` on this storyboard to write it. Do not generate a clip without its images.
2. Set Flow's output format to vertical 9:16 before generating any clip, and check it again before each one. Flow falls back to 16:9 on a new session.
3. Generate Video 1 in Flow's ingredients-to-video mode. Attach the Video 1 images for the route you pick in the 3x2 file: the six panel images (`Panel_1_V1.jpg` to `Panel_6_V1.jpg`) and the family reference image, which is seven images and the most Flow accepts for one clip, or the composed grid (`Grid_V1.jpg`) and the family reference image. Paste the Video 1 `Character locks:` block and then its three table rows, in order, as one prompt, VFX cells included. Do not generate the rows separately, do not leave the lock out, and do not set a start or an end frame.
4. Check the clip: vertical 9:16, and it matches its video's panel images in pose, expression, colour, tuft, size, head, framing, and light, each at its timecode. No Yeti changes colour, grows or shrinks, or picks up clothing partway through, and none of the grid's label text appears. The camera moves the way each `Camera:` sentence says, and no effect appears that its `Effects:` sentence does not name. If it drifts, regenerate the clip before continuing; do not fix it in the edit.
5. Generate Video 2 the same way, with its own images, its own `Character locks:` block, and its own table. Its first beat picks up the picture Video 1 ended on, under the same light. Apply one colour grade to the whole cut, matched to the palette in the `Lighting lock:` paragraph, so the three clips read as one film. Do not retime any clip; each one stays exactly 10 seconds.

Every video's table runs on its own 00:00 - 00:10 clock. Paste one video into Flow at a time: its `Character locks:` block and its table, and nothing else. This film was adapted from a source storyboard in which a boy and his dog are attacked by wolves and a small white creature lands in front of a glowing golden dragon. Four things were changed on purpose, and they stay changed. The boy is now a grown-up woodcutter, because a child alone in danger is what the safety filter refuses. The dog is now a small ginger tabby cat: an earlier cut of this film used a brown dog, and Flow could not keep a dog and three wolves apart, so the dog read as part of the pack and seemed to bark at the woodcutter with them. The cat differs from the wolves in every way the model sees and hears: small, bright orange, striped, riding on the bundle of sticks, and hissing and purring instead of barking. Nothing in this film barks. No wolf ever touches, bites, or tackles anyone: the woodcutter stumbles and sits down in soft snow, the cat puffs up and hisses and never fights, and the wolves keep their mouths closed and show no teeth. The glowing dragon behind the hero is gone, because a glowing creature or aura behind a character is cast energy, which the copyright filter reads as a famous film's power-up. In its place, as Babu Yeti lands, the cloud parts, a shaft of sunlight falls on him, and high above the pines the parting cloud curls into the shape of a dragon, edged in gold by the sun behind it. The dragon is weather: a real cloud, far up in the sky, lit from behind, grey-white with gold edges, never glowing from inside, never moving like a living creature, never touching or rising out of Babu Yeti. If a clip turns it into a glowing golden creature, an aura, or a dragon that springs from Babu Yeti, regenerate it.
