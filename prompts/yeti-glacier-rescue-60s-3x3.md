# Yeti Glacier Rescue 60s 3x3 Grids and Panel Images

Image prompts for `prompts/yeti-glacier-rescue-60s-storyboard.md`, which holds the video prompts. Each 10-second video has, under its own heading:

- One cinematic 3x3 timed storyboard prompt: one image with exactly nine panels, where row 1 is Shot 1, row 2 is Shot 2, and row 3 is Shot 3, and each panel is one beat of that shot's `Action:`, timed and framed by the panel map in section 6 of `.agents/rules/cinematic-direction.md`. It carries the locks as section 13 of `.agents/rules/character-consistency.md` sets out, with each Yeti's face given per panel in a `Faces:` list.
- Nine panel image prompts, one per grid panel, each drawing that panel's picture as a full single image with every lock, the row's light, and the panel's faces in the `[EXPRESSION]` slots.

Six grids and 54 panel images in total. The images are saved to `3x3-timed-storyboard-images/yeti-glacier-rescue/`: each grid as `Video N - <Title>.jpg` and each panel image as `Video N - <Title> - Panel P.jpg`. This film is widescreen, so every prompt opens with the storyboard's 16:9 line, and every panel and panel image is a 16:9 frame.

To make a video's images:

1. Generate the grid and the nine panel images one prompt at a time, each with the family reference image attached as a character reference, the image output set to landscape 16:9, and the prompt pasted whole. A project last used for the vertical Yeti films keeps its 9:16 setting, so check it before each generation.
2. Check every image against its prompt and against the look check in section 12 of `.agents/rules/character-consistency.md`: horizontal 16:9, each Yeti in its own colour with its own tuft, the baby Yeti about half as tall as a grown-up beside him, nothing worn and nothing on any head, no extra Yeti, the face the prompt names, the framing, angle, and light the prompt names, and every listed character and animal present and no one else. A grid also has nine panels in order with labels only in the strips above the pictures, and each panel image shows the same picture as its grid panel with no text in it. Regenerate any image that fails, and a grid with one failing panel whole; a wrong image is cheaper to redo than a wrong clip.
3. The adult fox appears in no image before panel 3 of Video 6. The kit stays wrapped in the grey blanket in the baby Yeti's arms in every image before Video 6, and no image shows a second small fox anywhere in the valley. Powder and ice dust settle on the blanket and the snow, never as a white coat on a Yeti's fur.
4. If the image model refuses a prompt, the filter causes in the storyboard's agent instructions apply to it too. Change only that prompt's picture and effects text and keep every lock. Row 2 of Video 3 and its panel images 4 to 6 are the likeliest trigger, because they look up at the falling slab from the hollow where the baby Yeti is. If the storyboard's fix for that shot is applied, rewrite row 2 of the Video 3 grid and panel images 4 to 6 to match it.

To use them in Google Flow, set the output format to landscape 16:9, generate the video's clip in ingredients-to-video mode with the video's table from the storyboard pasted as the prompt, and attach one of these:

- Grid route: the grid image and the family reference image.
- Panel route: panel images 3, 6, and 9, the picture each shot closes on.

Never set a grid or a panel image as a start or an end frame. Check the clip against the nine panels, each at its timecode.
