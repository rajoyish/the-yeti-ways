# Yeti Glacier Rescue 60s 3x2 Grids and Panel Images

Image prompts for `prompts/yeti-glacier-rescue-60s-storyboard.md`, which holds the video prompts. Each 10-second video has, under its own heading:

- One cinematic 3x2 timed storyboard prompt: one horizontal 16:9 image with exactly six panels, three across and two down, where panels 1 and 2 are Shot 1, panels 3 and 4 are Shot 2, and panels 5 and 6 are Shot 3, and each panel is one beat of that shot's `Action:`, timed and framed by the panel map in section 6 of `.agents/rules/cinematic-direction.md`. It carries the locks as section 13 of `.agents/rules/character-consistency.md` sets out, with each Yeti's face given per panel in a `Faces:` list.
- Six panel image prompts, one per grid panel, each drawing that panel's picture as a full single image with every lock, the shot's light, and the panel's faces in the `[EXPRESSION]` slots.

Six grids and 36 panel images in total. The images are saved to `3x2-timed-storyboard-images/yeti-glacier-rescue/`: each grid as `Video N - <Title>.jpg` and each panel image as `Video N - <Title> - Panel P.jpg`. This film is widescreen, so the grids, the pictures inside their panels, and the panel images are all horizontal 16:9, and every panel image opens with the storyboard's 16:9 line.

This file replaces `prompts/yeti-glacier-rescue-60s-3x3.md`, which drew nine panels per video from a storyboard with three beats per shot. The nine-panel grids and panel images in `3x3-timed-storyboard-images/yeti-glacier-rescue/` no longer match the storyboard, so none of them is attached to a clip. Generate the six-panel set below instead.

To make a video's images:

1. Generate the grid and the six panel images one prompt at a time, each with the family reference image attached as a character reference, the image output set to landscape 16:9, and the prompt pasted whole. A project last used for the vertical Yeti films keeps its 9:16 setting, so check it before each generation.
2. Check every image against its prompt and against the look check in section 12 of `.agents/rules/character-consistency.md`: horizontal 16:9, each Yeti in its own colour with its own tuft, the baby Yeti about half as tall as a grown-up beside him, nothing worn and nothing on any head, no extra Yeti, the face the prompt names, the framing, angle, and light the prompt names, and every listed character and animal present and no one else. A grid also has six panels, three across and two down, in order, with labels only in the strips above the pictures, and each panel image shows the same picture as its grid panel with no text in it. Regenerate any image that fails, and a grid with one failing panel whole; a wrong image is cheaper to redo than a wrong clip.
3. The adult fox appears in no image before panel 2 of Video 6. The kit stays wrapped in the grey blanket in the baby Yeti's arms in every image before Video 6, and no image shows a second small fox anywhere in the valley. The blue Yeti is in no panel of Video 2's Shot 3 (panels 5 and 6). Powder and ice dust settle on the blanket and the snow, never as a white coat on a Yeti's fur.
4. If the image model refuses a prompt, the filter causes in the storyboard's agent instructions apply to it too. Change only that prompt's picture and effects text and keep every lock. Panels 3 and 4 of Video 3 and their panel images are the likeliest trigger, because they look up at the falling slab from the hollow where the baby Yeti is. If the storyboard's fix for that shot is applied, rewrite panels 3 and 4 of the Video 3 grid and panel images 3 and 4 to match it.

To use them in Google Flow, set the output format to landscape 16:9, generate the video's clip in ingredients-to-video mode with the video's table from the storyboard pasted as the prompt, and attach one of these:

- Panel route: the six panel images and the family reference image. That is seven images, the most Flow accepts for one clip, and it gives Flow every beat of the clip at full size.
- Grid route: the grid image and the family reference image.

Never attach more than seven images, and never set a grid or a panel image as a start or an end frame. Check the clip against the six panels, each at its timecode.
