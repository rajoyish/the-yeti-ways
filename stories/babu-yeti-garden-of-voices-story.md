# Babu Yeti and the Garden of Voices

## Cast

- **Babu Yeti**: a little Yeti who loves to sing, grows proud of his sweet voice, and learns that every voice is lovely.
- **Mama Yeti**: his kind, wise mother, whom the forest friends ask to settle their quarrel.
- **Gombu Tortoise**: a slow, kind old tortoise with a low, humming voice, who tells Babu Yeti it is better when others praise you.
- **Mili Red Panda**: a gentle red panda who agrees with Gombu Tortoise.
- **Nimbu Tree Frog**: a chattery little tree frog who watches the quarrel from the old tree and wants the Rhino Chief to settle it.
- **Meadow Mouse**: three tiny mice in the glade; the littlest one wants Mama Yeti instead.
- **Sona Butterfly**: a golden butterfly who leads Mama Yeti to the glade.
- **Narrator**: the off-screen storyteller.

## Story

Once upon a time, high in a Himalayan forest of red rhododendron trees, there was a sunny glade full of wildflowers. In the middle of the glade sat a big flat stone, and every evening the forest friends gathered around it to share their songs. Gombu Tortoise hummed low and deep. Mili Red Panda whistled softly. The Meadow Mice drummed their tiny feet, and Nimbu Tree Frog chirruped from the branches of an old tree.

Babu Yeti loved to sing more than anything. He could trill high and sweet, like a little bamboo flute. But the more he sang, the prouder he grew of his own voice.

One golden evening, Babu Yeti climbed up onto the big flat stone, puffed out his fluffy chest, and boasted, "If it weren't for me, nobody here could sing a sweet song!"

The forest friends were stunned. The Meadow Mice's ears shot straight up. Gombu Tortoise blinked slowly. They all turned and looked at one another in surprise.

Then Gombu Tortoise spoke up, slow and kind. "Babu, it's better when others praise your voice for you. Many friends in this forest sing sweetly, too."

Babu Yeti's cheeks puffed up, and he stamped his foot on the stone. "Phooey! You can only hum! Why are you showing off?"

Mili Red Panda twitched her striped tail. "Babu, don't be so full of yourself. Everything Gombu said is completely right."

Because of Babu Yeti's sharp words, everyone began talking at once. The Meadow Mice squeaked, Mili Red Panda huffed, Gombu Tortoise grumbled, and a noisy quarrel filled the glade for a long, long while.

Up on a branch of the old tree, Nimbu Tree Frog had been listening to the whole quarrel. "Hey, everyone!" he called down. "Why are you fighting over nothing? Let's go to the Rhino Chief. He settles every quarrel!"

But the littlest Meadow Mouse did not like that idea at all. It squeaked that they should ask Mama Yeti instead, because she was kind and wise. And all the forest friends happily agreed.

The very next afternoon, Sona Butterfly fluttered ahead through the trees, leading Mama Yeti into the glade.

Mama Yeti looked around at all the forest friends and smiled. "Friends who sing sweetly never need to boast. Is anyone here proud like that?"

"No!" everyone cried together.

Then, in a small, quiet voice, Babu Yeti murmured, "Well... my voice is sweeter than everyone's."

Mama Yeti knelt down beside him and pointed to the meadow. "Babu, which garden do you like best? One with only one kind of flower, or one with many different kinds?"

Babu Yeti looked at the wildflowers, red and yellow, blue and white, purple and pink, all nodding together in the breeze. "I like a garden with many different kinds of flowers."

"Well said," Mama Yeti smiled. "In the same way, one voice alone doesn't stay sweet forever. Many different voices together sound sweet. That is why every voice here is lovely."

Mama Yeti's wise words touched Babu Yeti's heart, and at last he understood. He climbed down from the big flat stone and walked over to Gombu Tortoise and Mili Red Panda. "I'm sorry, Gombu. I'm sorry, Mili. Will you sing with me?"

Gombu Tortoise smiled a slow, kind smile. "Of course, Babu."

That evening, as the sun went down, the whole glade sang together: Gombu Tortoise's deep hum, Mili Red Panda's soft whistle, the Meadow Mice's drumming feet, Nimbu Tree Frog's chirrup, Mama Yeti's warm humming, and Babu Yeti's sweet little trill, all woven into one happy song. From that day on, Babu Yeti gave up his pride forever.

Let others praise your voice, and love every voice around you, because many voices together make the sweetest song of all.
