# Character Consistency Rules

## Purpose

This file controls only permanent character identity and visual continuity for Papa Yeti, Mama Yeti, and Babu Yeti.

It does not control environments, backgrounds, weather, time of day, actions, stories, dialogue, scene-specific lighting, transitions, duration, composition, or other production details that may naturally change between generations.

The following distinction must always be respected:

- **Immutable identity traits** define who the character is. They must remain identical in every generation and cannot be overridden by ordinary scene prompts.
- **Default visual styling traits** define the character’s normal presentation. They remain unchanged unless a prompt explicitly requests a temporary styling variation that does not alter identity.
- **Variable scene traits** include expressions, poses, actions, surroundings, and temporary interactions. They may change freely without changing facial identity, age, colour, body proportions, fur design, or distinguishing features.

## Global Character Consistency Rules

1. Always reproduce Papa Yeti, Mama Yeti, and Babu Yeti as the exact same three recognizable individuals in every generation.

2. Matching only the character’s colour, age, fur, clothing state, or general appearance is not sufficient. The generated result must look like the same individual, not a replacement or a newly designed similar-looking character.

3. Preserve each character’s exact facial identity, facial geometry, apparent age, role, body proportions, fur colour, face-and-paw colour, eye design, eyebrow design, nose design, mouth structure, head tuft, build, and distinguishing silhouette.

4. Expressions may move the eyebrows, eyelids, cheeks, and mouth naturally, but they must never reshape the underlying face, alter eye spacing, change muzzle width, modify the nose, distort the jaw, or produce a different identity.

5. Perspective, shading, rendering variation, and scene style must not alter facial geometry, body scale, fur colour, age, or recognizability.

6. Preserve the same fictional Yeti character type. Never turn any character into a human, ape, realistic animal, wild beast, horror creature, or conventional white snow monster.

7. Maintain the premium stylized 3D animated CGI appearance: friendly cartoon facial construction, soft rounded forms, and dense, individually rendered plush fur. The fur texture may be highly detailed, but the characters themselves must remain stylized rather than biologically realistic.

8. Each Yeti has one solid saturated fur colour from head to toe. Only the smooth face and paws use a lighter shade of that same character’s colour. Shading may create temporary light and dark values, but it must never create permanent patches, markings, a white belly, grey areas, or a second fur colour.

9. Each head is smooth, rounded, fully furred, and identified by one specific fur tuft. The tuft is the only distinct feature on top of the head. Never add horns, antlers, spikes, bumps, human hair, headwear, or other head structures.

10. The Yetis wear no clothing, hats, scarves, jewellery, bags, footwear, or permanent accessories. Their bodies are covered entirely by their own fur.

11. Paws have soft rounded fingers and toes without claws. Teeth, whenever visible, are clean, white, square, and softly rounded rather than pointed, fang-like, irregular, or realistic.

12. Do not beautify, age, de-age, masculinize, feminize, humanize, redesign, replace, merge, or create alternate versions of the characters.

13. Never transfer colours, facial features, hairstyles, tufts, body proportions, age traits, or distinguishing characteristics from one Yeti to another.

14. When a prompt conflicts with an immutable identity trait in this file, the identity lock takes priority.
